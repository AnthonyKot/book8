# Book 8 — Chapter template

Tagline (working): **Same event. Different classrooms.** Third line under discussion:
- (a) "Watch what each textbook needs the student to believe." — punchy, prosecutorial
- (b) "Every textbook tells the truth — about the countries it doesn't love." — hook + thesis + fairness promise

## The nine beats (every episode chapter)

1. **Kicker** — Event · year range · Chapter N.
2. **Lede** — one concrete image, no thesis. Sensory, dated, unarguable.
   Ex.: "In Guangzhou in 1839, chests of opium go into the sea…"
3. **The Question** — ONE sharp question per chapter, mapped to ONE coding category:
   - agency → "Who started it?" (Korea)
   - numbers → "Where did 22,000 people go?" (Katyn)
   - naming → "What do you call an invasion when you're marching in it?" (Sept 1939)
   - omission → "What happens where nobody's textbook looks?" (Hungary 1956)
   - justification → "Was it necessary?" (Hiroshima)
4. **The boring facts** — 5–8 lines of undisputed scaffolding (dates, places, counts,
   scholarly consensus). Labeled as such; deliberately dry — it's the ruler.
5. **The ladder** — side-by-side tellings ordered from most agentless to most agentive
   (never alphabetical). Cell = flag · edition year · original quote + translation ·
   one-line tag. The signature visual, repeated every chapter.
6. **The tell** — 2–3 ¶ of grammar-level analysis: passive voice, euphemism, scare
   quotes, justifying adjective, victory coda. No outrage — the quotes carry it.
6½. **The thread** (AGENT.md) — the one integrated causal story the exam units cut,
   + the personal hook (cup-of-tea rule), + "N of 11 classrooms tell any of this."
   General history, never attributed to a textbook; under a screen; wonder, not sneer.
7. **Credit where due** — which book handled THIS event best, and what it got right.
   The fairness engine paid in installments.
8. **Receipts** — page numbers, edition, cropped screenshot of the printed paragraph.
   Zero-mention findings as badges: "Катынь: 0 mentions in 241 pages."
9. **Coda** — same one line every chapter: "Now look up what your textbook calls it."
   The refrain and the shareable line.

## Worked example — Ch. ?: Korea, 1950–1953

- Lede: dawn artillery on the 38th parallel, a line drawn on a map five years earlier.
- Question: Who started it?
- Ladder (agentless → agentive): RU aid («Корейская война» — bare label) → CN («内战爆发»,
  then "the US invaded Korea") → IN ("broke out") → UA-1 ("North crossed the parallel and
  began the war") → UA-2 / UK / US ("North Korea, armed by the USSR, invaded the South").
- The tell: 抗美援朝 as war-title-as-verdict; scare quotes on "UN forces"; the victory coda.
- Credit: UA-1 for opening with both regimes wanting unification before naming who moved.
- Receipts: CN p. 175 (26课), UK ch. 8, US OpenStax §14, RU aid poster 21 answer key.
