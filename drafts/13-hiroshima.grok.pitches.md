# Chapter 13 (Pacific & Hiroshima) — Thread Pitches

### Pitch 1: The demonstration and the still-armed rival
- **Seed**: Italy states as fact that Truman’s decision “also had the purpose of offering… to the Soviet rival allies the demonstration of American military power” (IT ~p. 493); the UK calls it “a deliberate demonstration to the USSR” (UK p. 108). RU/BY/CN never stage that motive — the bomb is a military fact or co-cause of Japanese surrender, not a message to Moscow.
- **Loop**: The same weapon ends the Pacific war and opens a bipolar nuclear order → 1949 Soviet test ends the U.S. monopoly → arms race, crises, and “mutual assured destruction” become the grammar of great-power politics for the rest of the century.
- **Lander**: The word **nuclear** in ordinary speech — “nuclear option,” nuclear family as metaphor, nuclear winter as a weather of the mind — and the real arsenals still counted in every major capital the corpus countries read about.

### Pitch 2: Taiwan in the same paragraph as the bomb
- **Seed**: China’s single Hiroshima–Nagasaki sentence has no casualties and no Truman; the paragraph’s destination is 25 October 1945 — Chen Yi in Taipei, Taiwan “returned to the embrace of the motherland” (CN p. 165). No other ladder book ends the bomb story on that island.
- **Loop**: Japan’s collapse empties the empire’s Chinese and Taiwanese holdings → civil war resumes on the mainland → the unresolved status of Taiwan becomes a permanent fault line of the postwar Pacific, still wired into every U.S.–China–Japan security argument.
- **Lander**: The **Taiwan Strait** on today’s news maps — and the chip factories whose supply chains run through a reader’s phone in every corpus country.

### Pitch 3: Dual shock and the frozen Kurils
- **Seed**: Russia’s dual cause — “The atomic bombing of Japan by the USA and the entry of the USSR into the war forced… surrender” (RU p. 201) — and Belarus’s liberation list including the Kurils (BY p. 164). Anglo-American books either put Soviet entry after Hiroshima as a side clause (US p. 560) or treat the Russian advance as a reason the bombs were rushed (UK p. 108).
- **Loop**: Two shocks in three days (6 Aug bomb, 8–9 Aug Soviet war/entry, 9 Aug second bomb) produce Japanese surrender → Soviet occupation of southern Sakhalin and the Kurils → no Russo-Japanese peace treaty settles the islands.
- **Lander**: The **Kuril / Northern Territories** line still disputed on every honest map of Northeast Asia — a WWII border the reader can still find in the news.

### Pitch 4: The cities that are only a quiz answer
- **Seed**: Ukraine never names Hiroshima or Nagasaki in continuous narrative; the Japan section opens with a check question — “Which Japanese cities were subjected to atomic bombing?” — then jumps to the Missouri signing (UA §13). Eight of nine in-scope world-history books cover the cities by name; this is the avoidance signal.
- **Loop**: A syllabus that starts Japan’s story at occupation can teach Cold War nuclear anxiety, Chernobyl, and non-proliferation without ever narrating the first battlefield use of the weapon → the origin of the nuclear age becomes an assumed answer key rather than a told event.
- **Lander**: The **check-question format itself** — every adult who sat exams has filled a blank with a city name they were never asked to understand — plus the shared civilian nuclear scare word that *does* fill Ukrainian and post-Soviet pages: Chernobyl (1986), another cloud the same reader already knows.
