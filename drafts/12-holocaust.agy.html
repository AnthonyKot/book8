The Holocaust

On 20 January 1942, fifteen senior officials of the German Reich meet in a villa on the Grosser Wannsee in Berlin for a conference that lasts ninety minutes and ends with lunch. What each classroom does with the administrative machine of murder that followed is the subject of this chapter.

The question · agency

Who organized the murder — and how far down the state apparatus does your textbook trace the responsibility?

1 The boring facts

Dry scaffolding. These lines are the ruler; the ladder is the measurement.

  • 9–10 November 1938: the Kristallnacht pogroms across Germany, Austria, and the Sudetenland. Over 250 synagogues destroyed and roughly 30,000 Jewish men arrested and sent to concentration camps.
  • June 1941: following the invasion of the Soviet Union, mobile killing squads (Einsatzgruppen) of the SS and Police, assisted by Wehrmacht units and local collaborators, begin systematic mass shootings of Jewish populations.
  • 20 January 1942: the Wannsee Conference in Berlin coordinates state administrative machinery for the "Final Solution of the European Jewish Question" (Endlösung).
  • 1941–1945: industrial extermination camps operating gas chambers function in occupied Poland (Chełmno, Bełżec, Sobibór, Treblinka, Majdanek, Auschwitz-Birkenau).
  • Casualty figures: scholarly consensus estimates that 5.7 to 6 million Jews were murdered by Nazi Germany and its accomplices during the Holocaust, alongside millions of Soviet prisoners of war, Poles, Romani people, disabled individuals, and political prisoners.

Books outside the period — Kazakhstan's national history and China's national history — are in the receipts, not on the ladder. Kazakhstan is dedicated to national history and is never a world-event peer column here.

3 The tell

Every covering narrator in this set agrees on the scale: six million human beings, murdered across a continent. The disagreement is about where in the state structure the murder was ordered, and who stands alongside the dead.

Ukraine’s textbook (Гісем/Мартинюк 2019) places the six million dead in a single sentence on page 139. The passage does not name camps, extermination squads, or Wannsee bureaucrats. The murder is framed functionally: it is the immediate historical backdrop for the activation of the Zionist movement and the 1948 proclamation of Israel. The textbook returns to the Holocaust on page 108, but in the context of modern memory politics: Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán addressing Israel's parliament to condemn the crimes of the Holocaust and ask forgiveness for his nation's involvement. The tragedy is stated as a fact, then harnessed to statehood and diplomacy.

Russia’s unified course (Чубарьян 2023) defines the Holocaust as a "targeted policy of Nazi Germany, its allies and accomplices", but shifts to passive verbs when describing mass shootings on Soviet soil: after the capture of Kyiv, "about 70 thousand Jews were shot" in Babi Yar. The Russian narrative also performs a distinct nominative expansion: it immediately pairs the murder of six million Jews with the assertion that "Slavic peoples were also subjected to genocide", and counts 11 million destroyed in concentration camps ("Jews, Russians, Ukrainians, Poles") alongside 13.7 million Soviet civilians killed across occupied territories. The specificity of Jewish destruction is acknowledged, then folded into a broader national toll.

Italy and the United States move from single-dictator agency toward structural execution. Sabbatucci–Vidotto frame the project as conceived by Hitler in 1941 and "entrusted principally to the care of the SS", while noting that Italian Jews prefer the term Shoah over Holocaust due to the latter's sacrificial connotations. OpenStax introduces mobile execution units by name—the Einsatzgruppen—and explicitly broadens complicity beyond Germany, recording that systematic execution "required the collaboration of tens of thousands of people from across Europe." Lowe’s British textbook structures its narrative around historiographical debate, contrasting Intentionalists (who trace extermination to Hitler’s early ideology) with Functionalists (who see policy emerging from wartime circumstance), before settling on Ian Kershaw's verdict that Hitler's role was "decisive and indispensable."

Germany’s federal agency booklet (bpb Nr. 316) takes the self-incrimination pole. It explicitly rejects single-man attribution: "the Holocaust did not occur upon the sole decision of Hitler." Instead, it details how violence radicalized during the war of annihilation against the Soviet Union, stages the Wannsee Conference as a meeting where "representatives of ministries and Nazi bureaucracy deliberate on how the 'destruction of the Jews' is to be coordinated", documents SS doctors conducting selections on the ramp at Birkenau, and embeds the extermination program within Generalplan Ost (which planned the removal of 31 million Slavs). The German text does not treat murder as an aberration of one dictator, but as a coordinated enterprise of civilian ministries, state logistics, and Wehrmacht complicity.

The numbers across these columns carry a specific fight that must be handled with precision. Russia’s textbook states that "about 70 thousand Jews were shot" at Babi Yar. Germany’s booklet and Britain’s text record that "more than 33,000" or "some 34,000" were shot in the ravine in late September 1941. The spread is not a contradiction to be flattened: the German and British numbers count the initial two-day September massacre of Kyiv's Jewish population (33,771 per SS operational reports), while the Russian text applies the site's multi-year total across the entire occupation to the Jewish column alone.

Finally, two in-scope syllabuses present a striking silence. Belarus’s state monopoly textbook (Кошелев 2021, 11 кл.) covers World War II and fascism across multiple lessons, noting that Nazis used "anti-Semitic and patriotic slogans", but contains zero mentions of the Holocaust, concentration camps, gas chambers, or the mass murder of European Jews. India’s NCERT Class XI world history textbook marks Hitler’s 1933 rise to power and World War II on its timelines, but contains zero mentions of the Holocaust or Nazi death camps. Because six of the eight in-scope narrators cover the event (DE, UK, US, IT, RU, UA), the total absence of the Jewish genocide in the Belarussian and Indian classrooms represents an avoidance signal rather than a general curriculum norm.

4 Credit where due

Fairness, paid in installments

bpb Germany (DE) earns top credit for structural self-incrimination. It refuses to let responsibility rest on a single dictator, explicitly stating that the Holocaust did not occur on Hitler's sole decision, and meticulously documents the multi-agency coordination between civilian ministries, state bureaucracy, the SS, and Wehrmacht complicity.

OpenStax (US) deserves credit for naming European collaboration ("collaboration of tens of thousands of people from across Europe") and bringing the Einsatzgruppen, Wannsee bureaucracy, and non-Jewish victim totals into standard introductory survey prose.

Norman Lowe (UK) earns a nod for teaching students historiographical method by framing intentionalist versus functionalist debates before concluding with Kershaw's synthesis on Hitler's decisive role.

5 Where this stops being true

Limits

This ladder reflects six textbooks whose published scope covers the Second World War. The zero-mention status in Belarus (Кошелев 2021, 11 кл.) and India (NCERT Class XI) occurs in books whose period scope includes World War II; because six of eight in-scope books cover the Holocaust, these omissions are recorded as national avoidance signals.

Kazakhstan’s textbook (История Казахстана 2015) is dedicated to national history (covering 1937 Korean deportations and wartime Kazakh mobilization) and is not evaluated as a world-history peer column here. China’s state-compiled textbook (纲要 上 2019) focuses on national history (framing WWII around anti-Japanese resistance and the Nanjing Massacre). Sudan’s secondary history (2009) covers 1821–1948 regional history, noting Nazism only as a push factor for Jewish immigration to Palestine.

Discrepancies in Babi Yar death tolls (70,000 in RU vs ~33,000–34,000 in DE/UK) reflect different historical boundaries: German operational reports recorded 33,771 Jews executed on 29–30 September 1941, whereas Soviet state commission figures estimated total wartime casualties at the site (including POWs and hostages) at 70,000 or more. Readers seeking operational histories of individual camps or detailed regional survivor testimonies should consult specialized Holocaust historiography.
