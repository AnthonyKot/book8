### Pitch 1: Straight lines on the map app
- **Seed**: IT p. 144 — borders “traced on the geographic map… without taking any account of tribal divisions and of the pre-existing ethno-linguistic realities”; US p. 352 — Berlin allotted Africa “without input from Africans.”
- **Loop**: Berlin 1884–85 and bilateral deals turn claims into lines often aligned to meridians, rivers, or survey convenience → those lines become colonial borders → independence keeps the lines (OAU/AU uti possidetis logic) rather than redrawing by language or kingdom → the post-colonial state system inherits a cartography drawn for European claim, not African settlement.
- **Lander**: Open any map app to Africa: the long straight segments and right angles still on the screen are the Scramble’s most ordinary residue — shared by every reader who has ever pinched a continent.

### Pitch 2: The Suez artery that made Egypt worth occupying
- **Seed**: BY p. 77 — England in 1882 “occupied Egypt and thereby established control over the most important global artery — the Suez Canal”; UK’s Scramble paragraph (p. 26) names markets and raw materials but never the canal; SD lists Egypt 1882 / Sudan 1898 as sequential occupations.
- **Loop**: Canal opens 1869 and shortens Europe–Asia sea distance → Egypt’s debt and politics become European security questions → British occupation 1882 secures the artery → Sudan becomes the Nile hinterland of that security (Mahdist break, 1898 reconquest) → the “Scramble” inland is partly a fight over who sits on the route that still carries a large share of world container traffic.
- **Lander**: The same canal corridor on every shipping map and news graphic when a ship blocks it — ordinary proof that a nineteenth-century occupation still sits under global logistics. <!-- CHECK: share of world trade via Suez in recent years; Ever Given 2021 as optional news hook, not cause -->

### Pitch 3: Who gets a body count
- **Seed**: BY counts ~11 thousand Italians at Adwa and 28 thousand English dead in the Boer War; US counts 6,000 Italians killed at Adwa (the books disagree); SD alone counts African dead on its own side — ten thousand Ansar at El Obeid, 1882; Congo Free State atrocities described by US/IT without an aggregate African death total in these school pages.
- **Loop**: School history numbers the European battlefield dead of colonial wars → African combat deaths and forced-labor deaths stay unnumbered or local → the Scramble enters memory as a diplomatic race with European casualty footnotes → only the African narrator’s long unit reverses the tally by mourning its own army first.
- **Lander**: The habit of “how many died?” that every news reader brings to a war — and the recognition that most classrooms applied that habit only to one side of this one.

### Pitch 4: Fashoda and the alliance that fought 1914
- **Seed**: IT alone stages Fashoda 1898 in detail (British reconquest column meets French fort on the Nile; France withdraws); RU/BY narrate Anglo-French African demarcation and the 1904 Entente without the fort; UK Scramble paragraph never reaches Fashoda.
- **Loop**: Nile collision nearly makes France and Britain enemies over African interior lines → diplomatic climb-down and sphere deals → colonial settlement becomes a plank of the Entente Cordiale → the same two powers enter 1914 as partners, having almost fought each other over a Sudanese reach of river.
- **Lander**: Causal precision: Fashoda did not cause World War I — it helped clear the colonial quarrel that let the Franco-British alliance form. The lander is the word “entente” still used for any wary partnership, with a Nile near-war in its African backstory. <!-- CHECK: Entente Cordiale 8 April 1904 terms on Egypt/Morocco -->
