# Chapter 11 — Munich → Pact → September 1939: Thread Pitches

Per `AGENT.md` guidelines, the thread beat is pitched before drafting so the reader can select the candidate that delivers the strongest personal hook and shared loop. Below are four candidates grounded in the comparison findings of Chapter 11.

---

### Candidate 1: "Appeasement" as Living Political Vocabulary (Linguistic Lander)

- **Seed**: UK, US, IT, and DE books center "appeasement" (*appeasement*, *politica di appeasement*, *Appeasement-Politik*) as the defining error of 1938, while RU and BY label Munich a "collusion" («сговор») and "cajoling the aggressor."
- **The Loop**: Neville Chamberlain intended *appeasement* as a positive diplomatic term for settling legitimate grievances without war; its catastrophic failure at Munich transformed the word into a permanent geopolitical curse used across post-1945 history (from Suez and Korea to Vietnam and modern Eastern Europe) to shut down negotiation.
- **Shared Hook**: A word every reader uses or hears on the news today whenever a leader sits down to negotiate with an international rival.

### Candidate 2: The 1921 Curzon Line on Today's European Map (Material / Border Lander)

- **Seed**: RU (pp. 158–159) alone explicitly invokes Lord Curzon and the 1921 Treaty of Riga to justify the Red Army's September 17 entry ("liberation of Western Ukraine and Western Belorussia"), while Western textbooks treat the border shift purely as Molotov-Ribbentrop territorial partition.
- **The Loop**: British Foreign Secretary Lord Curzon proposed an ethnographic demarcation line in 1919; Poland rejected it after victory in the 1920 Polish-Soviet War; Stalin reclaimed it in the secret protocols of 1939 and had it cemented at Yalta in 1945 as the permanent frontier.
- **Shared Hook**: Today's eastern border of Poland (and the eastern border of the European Union and NATO with Belarus and Ukraine)—a line millions of travelers and goods cross every day—is the exact line drawn by a British lord in 1919 and enforced in September 1939.

### Candidate 3: September 17 on Today's Calendar: National Unity Day (Calendar / Memory Lander)

- **Seed**: BY (p. 158) alone records: *"This day in Belarus became a state holiday — National Unity Day"* («Этот день в Беларуси стал государственным праздником — Днем народного единства»), celebrating the Red Army's September 17 entry as the reunification of the Belarusian nation.
- **The Loop**: While Poland and NATO nations remember September 17, 1939 as the date of Soviet invasion and partition, Belarus instituted September 17 in 2021 as an official public holiday celebrating state completion and anti-colonial reunification.
- **Shared Hook**: A date on today's calendar where people living on one side of an EU border enjoy a public day off to celebrate an event that neighbors across the border mark as a day of national mourning.

### Candidate 4: The "Munich Analogy" & The Architecture of Post-War Treaties (Structural Lander)

- **Seed**: OpenStax (p. 531) explicitly highlights *"The lessons learned from Hitler's violation of the Munich Pact"* as the origin of modern defense guarantees, whereas Eastern books view Munich as proof that Western guarantees were worthless.
- **The Loop**: The belief that making territorial concessions to an expansionist power only guarantees a wider war later became the hardwired strategic doctrine behind NATO's Article 5 and post-WWII collective security architecture.
- **Shared Hook**: The diplomatic slogan *"No More Munichs"* that underpins modern treaty commitments and military interventions whenever borders are challenged.
