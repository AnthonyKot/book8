# Candidate Thread Pitches — Chapter 7: China 1911–1949

### Pitch 1: The Hyperinflation Lander (The Banknote That Bought a Single Grain of Rice)
- **Seed**: OpenStax (US) alone notes that Nationalist prices rose 30% per month between 1945 and 1949, alienating the middle class, while CN frames the defeat strictly as ideological and moral collapse.
- **The Loop**: Printing fiat money without tax revenue led to 60-billion-yuan single banknotes → urban salary workers, merchants, and soldiers lost their life savings in weeks → the urban middle class abandoned Chiang Kai-shek without a single shot fired on their street.
- **Shared Lander**: Banknotes in hyperinflation (a paper object every modern reader recognizes from Weimar Germany, Zimbabwe, or modern inflation stories).
- **Who Tells It**: 1 of 11 classrooms (US alone) names the financial mechanism that broke Nationalist urban support.

### Pitch 2: The Silver Standard Loop (How a US Senate Subsidizing Nevada Unmade China)
- **Seed**: Western and Soviet textbooks treat Chiang's 1928–1937 decade as a military struggle against warlords and Red soviets, omitting why rural China collapsed economically in 1934 just as the Red Army launched the Long March.
- **The Loop**: US Congress passed the Silver Purchase Act of 1934 to subsidize Western US silver miners → the US Treasury bought global silver at above-market prices → silver drained out of China's silver-standard economy → massive monetary contraction, credit collapse, and farm bankruptcies across rural China → Chiang lost his tax base while desperate peasants joined the Red Army.
- **Shared Lander**: Silver coins / commodity money (an American legislative vote in Washington destroying the currency of rural China, forcing China onto fiat paper and setting up the 1945–1949 hyperinflation).
- **Who Tells It**: 0 of 11 classrooms connect Roosevelt's 1934 Silver Purchase Act to the economic window of the Long March.

### Pitch 3: The Warlord Railway Deficit (The European Loan That Unmade the Qing Dynasty)
- **Seed**: Sabbatucci–Vidotto (IT) alone notes that a 1911 government railway nationalization scheme "provoked a series of uprisings" leading to Sun Yat-sen's assembly, while CN focuses on Wuchang military conspirators.
- **The Loop**: The Qing dynasty signed a loan contract with a European four-power banking syndicate (UK, France, Germany, US) to nationalize provincial railway lines → local Chinese merchants and gentry lost their investments without compensation → the Railway Protection Movement erupted in Sichuan → Qing transferred Wuchang garrison troops to Sichuan → left Wuchang empty, allowing the Eighth Engineering Battalion to fire the first shot on October 10.
- **Shared Lander**: Foreign infrastructure loans / railway bonds (a financial contract signed in London and Paris breaking a two-thousand-year imperial dynasty).
- **Who Tells It**: 1 of 11 classrooms (IT) mentions the railway spark; 0 connect the European banking consortium directly to the empty barracks at Wuchang.

### Pitch 4: The Grammatical Lander ("People's Liberation" vs "Civil War" in the UN Seat)
- **Seed**: Ukraine and Italy note that Taiwan held China's UN seat until 1971, while CN names the 1946–1949 campaign the "People's War of Liberation" (解放战争).
- **The Loop**: Naming a war a "civil war" preserves state continuity (one state with two internal factions), whereas naming it a "War of Liberation" asserts that the pre-existing state was an illegitimate occupying regime → the UN vote in 1971 (Resolution 2758) was not merely a diplomatic realignment, but the international adoption of the CCP's grammatical verdict over the GMD's.
- **Shared Lander**: The UN Security Council seat / voting board (a global institution every reader sees in international news today).
- **Who Tells It**: 3 of 11 classrooms (UA, IT, US) note the UN seat discrepancy; 1 of 11 (CN) uses the "Liberation" title.
