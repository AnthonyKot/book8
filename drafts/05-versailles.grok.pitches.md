### Pitch 1: The empty chair *(material — seed from the ladder — recommended)*

- **Seed (from the ladder).** OpenStax opens the conference with a parenthesis: leaders of the major Allied powers “(except Russia)” met at Versailles (US, p. 476). Russia’s state book does the same fact as a wound, not a footnote: “Soviet Russia was not represented at the Paris Peace Conference and was not only removed from the creation of the system of the post-war peace settlement, but also became an object of intervention by the Western powers” (RU, ~p. 54). Belarus’s Big Three never need a fourth European empire at the table; Italy notes the defeated “not represented at the conference” without naming Moscow as the missing victor-side power.
- **Loop.** 1917–18 takes Russia out of the Allied war as Tsarist partner and returns it as a revolutionary state the victors will not seat → Paris writes the European and colonial map without a Russian signature → intervention and later isolation sit next to German “war guilt” in the same system → the order of the 1920s is an order whose authors left one great power’s chair empty on purpose.
- **Lander.** Every later argument about “who was at the table” when a border was drawn is borrowing this shape. The empty chair is not a metaphor first — it is a seating plan, still legible whenever a peace process publishes a guest list.
- **Who tells it.** 2 of 11 name Russia’s absence at the conference opening or fragility section (US, RU); 0 of 11 of the German-objections belt (UK) put the empty chair next to the Diktat debate; CN/SD/IN have other absences (justice, Arabs, thin fuse) and do not need Moscow’s seat.
- **Receipts-lite.** Paris Peace Conference membership lists; Allied intervention in the Russian Civil War, 1918–20; separate US–German peace, 1921. <!-- CHECK: intervention chronology; US–German Treaty of Berlin 25 Aug 1921 -->
- **Why it fits this chapter.** Earned by the ladder’s US/RU pair; seeds the Part II→V braid (order-making without a power that will later remake it); causal precision is easy: the empty chair did not cause the next war — it produced a settlement whose authors did not include a state that still had to live next to it. Feeds Munich/Pact wiring (ch. 11) without stealing that chapter’s job.

### Pitch 2: Qingdao on the map *(material — feeds ch. 7)*

- **Seed (from the ladder).** CN never titles the treaty Versailles; the injury is geographic and named: Germany’s rights in Shandong transferred to Japan; students shout “还我青岛” / “Return our Qingdao”; Chinese delegates refuse to sign (CN, ~pp. 136–137). OpenStax: “Without consulting the millions of Chinese people living there… Japan to retain this territory” until 1922 (US, p. 479). Italy: China “sacrificed… Shantung” (IT, ~p. 456). IN: territories not returned, three sentences, no place-name (IN, ~p. 168).
- **Loop.** Germany held Jiaozhou / Qingdao as a concession → Japan seizes it in the war and keeps it at Paris → a victorious China is told power beats justice → May Fourth turns a diplomatic page into a national political generation → the same peninsula is still a place on every phone map, still a port city, still the name on the slogan.
- **Lander.** Type **Qingdao** (or Tsingtao) into a map app. The beer brand that still uses the old German spelling is a commercial fossil of the concession era; the city is not a fossil. <!-- CHECK: German lease of Jiaozhou Bay 1898; Tsingtao brewery founded 1903 under German administration; Japan returns Shandong sovereignty under 1922 Washington agreements -->
- **Who tells it.** 3 of 11 name Shandong/Shantung as Paris cargo (CN, US, IT); 1 of 11 has May Fourth without the peninsula (IN); 0 of 11 in the UK German-objections seminar need Qingdao; SD’s 1919 is Arab, not Chinese.
- **Receipts-lite.** Treaty clauses on Shandong; Chinese delegation non-signature, 28 June 1919; Washington Conference settlement of Shandong, 1922. <!-- CHECK: Nine-Power / Shandong agreements Feb 1922 -->
- **Why it fits.** Cleanest lander and cleanest feed into ch. 7. Risk: can read as “the China chapter early” if the comparison’s multi-grievance finding (RU empty chair, SD mandates) is not kept in view. Causal precision: Shandong did not “cause” the Chinese Communist Party — it produced the fuse CN’s own lesson names for May Fourth.

### Pitch 3: Diktat / war guilt as words still in the mouth *(linguistic — inherits ch. 4)*

- **Seed (from the ladder).** UK alone stages the German term as a debate item: peace as a “Diktat,” Article 231 / War Guilt, Hitler’s later claim it should not be “morally binding,” countered with Brest-Litovsk (UK, ~pp. 69–72). IT uses the same German word as definition: “un Diktat… imposizione” (IT, ~p. 296). RU: Article 231, Germany “sole culprit,” then “war guilt” as part of the time bomb (RU, ~pp. 52, 54). BY: “all responsibility… placed upon Germany,” then humiliation → fascism (BY, pp. 99, 103). Ch. 4 already showed BY redistributing 1914 guilt away from the Versailles verdict.
- **Loop.** 1919 needs a liable party for reparations and disarmament → Article 231 puts war responsibility in the treaty text → schoolbooks either restage German objections (UK), package guilt as time bomb (RU/BY), or skip the clause because their 1919 is Shandong or San Remo → the portable words **Diktat** and **war guilt** outlive the treaty as names for any imposed settlement and any forced confession.
- **Lander.** “War guilt” and “dictated peace” still turn up in ordinary political argument whenever a loser is told to sign first and talk later. The reader has heard the shape even if they have never heard Article 231.
- **Who tells it.** 4 of 11 put war guilt / Diktat / sole responsibility in the settlement lesson (UK, IT, RU, BY); US names “war guilt” clause; CN/SD/IN: 0 for Article 231 in these passages.
- **Receipts-lite.** Treaty of Versailles, Article 231 (28 June 1919); Keynes, *The Economic Consequences of the Peace* (1919) as the named dissent Lowe channels. <!-- CHECK: Keynes 1919 publication; Article 231 wording -->
- **Why it fits.** Tightest braid from ch. 4’s “who started it?” into ch. 5’s paper verdict, and forward toward Munich revision (ch. 11). Risk: stacking two chapters on the same noun pair; the lander is linguistic, not a pocket object. Causal precision: Article 231 did not invent German nationalism — it gave revision a clause number.

### Pitch 4: “A new link in the chain” *(my candidate — material / linguistic — SD signature)*

- **Seed (from the ladder).** Sudan alone routes 1919 through Arab self-determination and San Remo, not German reparations: peoples “clung” to Wilson’s self-determination; Britain, France, and Italy “clung to their colonial interests”; mandate as a system “devised by shrewd English politicians”; punchline — Arab countries became subject to “a new link in the chain of colonialism” (SD, ~pp. 197–198). RU’s “colonial booty” is the only other column that puts empire in the fragility paragraph; it does not name San Remo or Arab self-determination. UK/BY/IT German lessons can list mandates as German/Ottoman spoils without making them the subject.
- **Loop.** Wartime promises and Wilsonian language raise expectation of independence → San Remo converts Ottoman Arab provinces into Class A mandates under Britain and France → the word **mandate** teaches trusteeship while the map teaches partition → borders and place-names from that settlement (Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, Palestine, Transjordan) remain the ordinary nouns of news maps a century later.
- **Lander.** Open a news map of the eastern Mediterranean. The country names and the argument over who drew them are still the San Remo generation’s furniture — whether or not any textbook in the reader’s country ever used the word mandate. <!-- CHECK: Class A mandates under League Covenant art. 22; San Remo decisions Apr 1920 -->
- **Who tells it.** 1 of 11 builds 1919 as Arab mandate betrayal (SD); 1 of 11 names colonial booty at Paris without the Arab classroom (RU); 0 of 11 in the German-objections belt make San Remo the chapter’s hinge.
- **Receipts-lite.** San Remo Conference 1920; League of Nations mandate texts; Sykes–Picot 1916 as wartime background SD already teaches. <!-- CHECK: Sykes–Picot 16 May 1916 -->
- **Why it fits.** Non-obvious for readers raised on “Versailles = German humiliation”; shared lander (map still in use); signature-topic energy without claiming most books “hid” the Middle East. Causal precision: the mandate system did not invent Middle Eastern conflict — it produced the legal-colonial link SD names, under which those borders were administered. Risk: can pull the chapter off the German/Chinese comparison if not kept under a screen and tied to RU’s booty line as the thin second witness.
