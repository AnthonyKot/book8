# Chapter 6 (1917) — Thread Pitches

### Pitch 1: The Sealed Train & the Finnish Station Ticket
- **Seed**: OpenStax (US, p. 464) notes "Germany helped him return," while Russian and Belarusian textbooks omit Imperial Germany's transit logistics entirely.
- **Loop**: Facing a devastating two-front war of attrition, the German High Command funds anti-war agitation and transports Lenin across enemy territory in a sealed train carriage from Switzerland to Petrograd → Lenin arrives at Finland Station, dismantles Bolshevik support for the Provisional Government, and forces the Brest-Litovsk peace → Soviet Russia surrenders its western territories, enabling Germany to shift fifty divisions to the Western Front and triggering the Allied emergency that pulled American troops into Europe.
- **Lander**: The physical railway route and ticket that exchanged one frontline collapse for America's permanent entry into European geopolitics.

### Pitch 2: The Petrograd Bread Queue & International Women's Day
- **Seed**: Koshelev (BY, p. 106) notes "interruptions in the supply of bread," and Lowe (UK, p. 514) records "bread riots," while eight other textbooks treat February as an abstract constitutional collapse.
- **Loop**: War Requisitioning broke Petrograd's civilian supply lines, turning sub-zero flour shortages into multi-day street queues on International Women's Day → 100,000 striking women textile workers and housewives marched for bread, fraternizing with garrison troops ordered to shoot them → military mutiny turned food riots into total imperial collapse in seven days → three centuries of Romanov autocracy ended not in battlefield defeat, but in a bakery line.
- **Lander**: International Women's Day (March 8) and the 8-hour workday, global civic standards that trace their modern placement to the Petrograd bread queue of February 1917.

### Pitch 3: The Comintern Network & the Word "Cadre"
- **Seed**: NCERT (IN, p. 171) highlights that "Lenin and Trotsky went on to establish the Comintern," and 纲要 (CN, p. 143) records that Comintern delegates "jointly discussed party-building," while Western books confine 1917 to a domestic Russian coup.
- **Loop**: October's victory transformed Petrograd into the command hub of the Third International (1919), exporting organizational manuals and party funding across Asia, Africa, and the Americas → Chinese, Indian, and African anti-colonial movements adopted Bolshevik vanguard architecture over Western parliamentary models → post-colonial state-building across the Global South was structured around disciplined party apparatuses.
- **Lander**: The organizational vocabulary spoken across global politics today ("cadre", "politburo", "party line", "cell").
