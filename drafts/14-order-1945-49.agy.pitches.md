# Chapter 14 Pitch Candidates — The 1945–1949 order

### Candidate 1: The Counterpart Funds (How Marshall Aid Built Europe's State Investment Engines)
- **Seed**: UA notes Marshall aid ($13 billion / $125 billion modern) carried a prerequisite of removing communists from cabinet; US cites $12 billion ($147 billion modern); BY calls aid "conditioned by political demands" giving the US powerful levers.
- **Loop**: US post-WWII farm and factory overproduction threatened a return to 1930s deflation → the Marshall Plan shipped American agricultural and industrial surplus directly to European governments → European states sold these goods to local buyers and deposited the national-currency proceeds into mandatory state "counterpart funds" used for infrastructure and industrial investment (such as Germany's KfW bank) → built the integrated transatlantic market while shutting out the Soviet sphere.
- **Lander**: Public state investment banks still operating today (like Germany's KfW) or European agricultural import standards.
- **Who tells it**: 5 of 11 classrooms name the Marshall Plan; 0 of 11 explain counterpart funds or how US agricultural surplus clearing financed European state reconstruction.

### Candidate 2: The Theater Safety Drop (How an Anti-Fire Wall Became Geopolitics)
- **Seed**: IT quotes Churchill at Fulton ("an iron curtain has descended"); US and UK highlight the phrase; BY calls Fulton a "warlike speech."
- **Loop**: Nineteenth-century European theaters were required by law to install a heavy iron drop-curtain between stage and auditorium to prevent backstage fires from incinerating the audience → George Orwell borrowed the theatrical technical term in October 1945 to describe the atomic division of the world → Winston Churchill adopted it at Fulton in March 1946 → Walter Lippmann turned it into the vocabulary of daily news.
- **Lander**: The physical iron safety curtain still tested before performances in traditional theaters worldwide.
- **Who tells it**: 5 of 11 classrooms cite Churchill's Fulton speech; 2 mention Orwell or Lippmann (UA, IT); 0 mention that the "iron curtain" was a piece of theater safety technology built to contain fire.

### Candidate 3: Operation Little Vittles & Airport Radar (How the Berlin Airlift Built Air Traffic Control)
- **Seed**: UA records 2.3 million tons of cargo airlifted; UK records 2 million tons; US describes ground route cutoffs; BY omits the blockade entirely.
- **Loop**: Supplying 2.5 million West Berliners by air required landing a cargo plane every 45 to 90 seconds in fog, rain, and narrow flight corridors → US and British flight operations deployed Ground-Controlled Approach (GCA) radar and minute-by-minute flight slot scheduling → converted wartime radar into the standardized civilian air traffic control system that manages commercial flights today.
- **Lander**: Ground-controlled approach radar and standard civilian flight scheduling used by every commercial airport.
- **Who tells it**: 4 of 11 classrooms cover the Berlin Airlift (UA, IT, UK, US); 1 in-scope covering book omits it (BY); 0 connect the blockade logistics to modern civil air traffic control.

### Candidate 4 (Non-obvious): COCOM & The Split Grid (How Dual-Use Embargoes Built Incompatible Europes)
- **Seed**: US, UK, and UA highlight the founding of NATO in April 1949 and the emergence of competing economic blocs (OEEC vs CMEA).
- Loop: In late 1949, Western allies created COCOM (Coordinating Committee for Multilateral Export Controls) to block dual-use technology—turbines, ball bearings, radar, and specialty steel—from reaching the Soviet bloc → forced Soviet engineers to construct incompatible industrial standards, pipeline diameters, and electrical grid coupling specifications → physically carved Europe into two technical systems that outlived the Soviet Union itself.
- **Lander**: High-voltage grid coupling stations and pipeline gauge adapters along the former East-West border.
- **Who tells it**: 0 of 11 classrooms mention COCOM or technical infrastructure embargoes, presenting the Cold War division purely as a war of political manifestos.
