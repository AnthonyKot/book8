# Chapter 15: Korea — Thread Candidates Pitch Round

Presented to the reader before drafting the integrated-history thread for Chapter 15 (*Korea — the finale*). Per `AGENT.md` rules, each candidate lands in ≤4 sentences, identifying the comparison seed, the causal loop, the shared hook, and the dossier classroom count.

### Candidate (a): The Half-Hour Border
- **Seed**: The 38th parallel appears in all six non-agentless textbooks (US, UK, IT, UA, CN, IN), but none explain who drew it or why.
- **The Loop**: On the night of August 10, 1945, with Soviet troops entering northern Korea and no US forces nearby, two young US officers—Dean Rusk and Charles Bonesteel—were given thirty minutes to partition the peninsula. Using a small 1942 *National Geographic* wall map, they chose the 38th parallel because it placed Seoul in the US zone while keeping the boundary simple; Soviet commanders unexpectedly agreed without bargaining. That rushed half-hour decision created the most heavily militarized border on earth.
- **Shared Hook**: Every reader holding a modern smartphone (Samsung, LG, or Apple components) holds hardware manufactured in tech parks situated less than 50 kilometers south of that 1945 wall-map line.
- **Classroom Count**: 0 of 11 textbooks explain how or by whom the 38th parallel was chosen.

### Candidate (b): The War That Never Ended
- **Seed**: Ukraine’s textbook (*Hisem & Martyniuk 2019*, p. 172) drops a poignant legal observation: *“Although it was possible to stop it, the parties are still in a state of war.”*
- **The Loop**: The 1953 Panmunjom agreement was an armistice—a cease-fire between military commanders—not a peace treaty between sovereign states. South Korean President Syngman Rhee refused to sign it, while the US signed under the UN Command flag. Because no peace treaty ever followed, the 1950 war never legally closed, freezing two million soldiers in permanent active readiness for over seventy years.
- **Shared Hook**: Every news alert about missile tests, DMZ border standoffs, or joint military exercises is not a new conflict, but an active battle station of a single 1950 war that was paused but never ended.
- **Classroom Count**: 1 of 11 textbooks (Ukraine) notes the ongoing technical state of war; 10 of 11 present 1953 as a simple end date.

### Candidate (c): Koryo-saram — The Lost Deportation
- **Seed**: Kazakhstan’s national history textbook skips the 1950–53 Korean War battlefield entirely, but devotes its Korean pages to the 1937 NKVD forced deportation of 172,000 Far Eastern Koreans to the Kazakh steppe.
- **The Loop**: Fearing Japanese espionage before WWII, Stalin ordered the entire Korean population of Vladivostok and the Soviet Far East deported by boxcar to Central Asia. Out of that displaced diaspora (*Koryo-saram*) grew a unique Soviet-Korean culture that produced iconic figures like Soviet rock pioneer Viktor Tsoi (Kino).
- **Shared Hook**: Music and culture from post-Soviet Central Asia to Seoul still trace their ancestry to a 1937 railway clearing.
- **Honesty Flag / Test**: *May fail the shared-across-corpus test.* While deeply resonant in post-Soviet countries (RU, UA, BY, KZ), readers outside Eurasia likely do not know Viktor Tsoi or Koryo-saram history.

### Candidate (d): The Institutionalized Fiction ("Volunteers" and Fake Uniforms)
- **Seed**: China’s textbook explicitly names the *“Chinese People’s Volunteers”* («中国人民志愿军»); Italy and the UK put *“volunteers”* in scare quotes; Ukraine notes *“Soviet military advisors.”*
- **The Loop**: To prevent the Korean conflict from escalating into World War III, all three major powers adopted institutionalized fictions: the US fought under a borrowed UN flag rather than declaring war; China dispatched 300,000 regular troops rebranded as unofficial "Volunteers"; and Moscow deployed elite MiG-15 jet fighter regiments whose Russian pilots wore Chinese uniforms, flew with Chinese markings, and were forbidden to speak Russian over the radio.
- **Shared Hook**: The modern international convention of "proxy war" and "unmarked troops"—visible in modern conflicts from Crimea to the Middle East—was codified over the skies of "MiG Alley" in 1951.
- **Classroom Count**: 2 of 11 textbooks (UK, IT) use scare quotes on "volunteers"; 0 of 11 mention the undercover Soviet MiG fighter pilots.
