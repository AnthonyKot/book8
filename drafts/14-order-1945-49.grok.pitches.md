### Pitch 1: “Iron curtain” still in the mouth *(linguistic — seed from the ladder)*

- **Seed (from the ladder).** Italy and the United Kingdom print Churchill’s line almost whole: “From Stettin… to Trieste… an iron curtain has descended…” (IT ~p. 502; UK ~p. 129). OpenStax shortens it to “an iron curtain” “trapping half the continent under Soviet domination” (US p. 574). Ukraine stages Fulton as a call to struggle against communism and prints Stalin’s reply that the speech is a “war-kindler,” but never needs the metal-curtain phrase itself (UA pp. 15–16). Belarus makes Fulton the beginning of the Cold War and calls the speech “warlike” — still no curtain (BY p. 167). Russia never reaches Fulton.
- **Loop.** A wartime ally names a closed border with a stage-prop metaphor → the metaphor travels into every Western survey as the portable image of the freeze → eastern European ministry books that teach the same years can stage the speech as aggression without borrowing the English metaphor → half a century later the same two words are still the default headline for any sealed frontier.
- **Lander.** “Iron curtain” is still ordinary political speech for a border you cannot cross and a news blackout behind it. The reader has heard the phrase applied to later walls and later regimes long before anyone mentioned Fulton, Missouri.
- **Who tells it.** 3 of 11 print the curtain phrase (IT, UK, US); 2 of 11 stage Fulton without it (UA, BY); 0 of 11 explain why a theatre curtain was the right metaphor for a customs line and a police state. <!-- CHECK: Churchill had used “iron curtain” earlier; the Fulton speech made it global -->
- **Why it fits.** Cleanest shared lander across corpus countries; risk is familiarity (everyone thinks they already know the phrase). Causal precision: the speech did not create the occupation zones — it named a line already drawn on the ground and made the name portable.

### Pitch 2: Marshall money as condition, not gift *(material — seed from the comparison)*

- **Seed (from the ladder).** Ukraine alone among the six covering books puts the price and the politics in adjacent sentences: ~$13 billion, and “a preliminary condition for providing aid was the removal of communists from the composition of governments,” plus the US need to shed surplus capital and build solvent markets (UA p. 17). Belarus drops the dollar total and keeps the politics: assistance “conditioned by political demands,” after which “the USA received powerful levers of influence on Europe” (BY pp. 167–168). Lowe prints Molotov’s counter-name — “dollar imperialism” — and the Soviet refusal that kept eastern Europe off the list (UK ~p. 130). OpenStax’s $12 billion also “helped ensure that the U.S. economy did not slide into an economic depression” (US p. 576). Russia never reaches Marshall at all.
- **Loop.** Western Europe needs coal, food, and dollars after 1945 → Washington offers reconstruction money that is also a market and a political filter → Moscow reads the filter as a sphere-of-influence raid and forbids “satellites” to accept → the same dollars become recovery in one classroom and levers in another → the figure (~$12–13 billion) is what the student remembers; the condition is what the ladder actually splits on.
- **Lander.** “Marshall Plan” is still the template name for any big reconstruction package with strings — journalists reach for it after wars and financial crashes the reader has lived through. The metaphor outlived the European Recovery Program.
- **Who tells it.** 4 of 11 name Marshall dollars or the plan (UA, BY, IT, UK, US — five if IT’s 13 miliardi counts); 2 of 11 stress the political condition/levers (UA, BY); 1 of 11 prints “dollar imperialism” (UK); 0 of 11 join condition + surplus markets + Molotov’s insult in one paragraph.
- **Receipts-lite.** Marshall’s Harvard speech, 5 June 1947; Economic Cooperation Act 1948; any standard total for ERP grants/loans. <!-- CHECK: dollar totals vary by counting method; keep the corpus’s own figures as data -->
- **Why it fits.** The hinge is earned entirely by the comparison (UA/BY vs UK/US temperature on the same money). Causal precision: the Plan did not “start” the Cold War alone — it is what made recovery and alignment the same signature.

### Pitch 3: The system only two classrooms name *(linguistic / institutional — my non-obvious candidate)*

- **Seed (from the ladder / silence).** Ukraine and Belarus alone install a proper noun: the “Yalta-Potsdam system of international relations” (UA p. 10; BY pp. 10, 164). Italy, the UK, and the US narrate the same arc — Yalta → Fulton → Truman → Marshall → Berlin → NATO — as a sequence of crises and doctrines without ever giving the order a system name. Russia reaches Yalta and Potsdam as settlement conferences and stops. The finding is not that the West “hides” Yalta; it is that only the post-Soviet ministry pair needs the freeze to be a *named order* with a birth certificate.
- **Loop.** Two conferences produce occupation maps, sphere language, and a UN charter → later curricula must decide whether those meetings founded a *system* or merely opened a *crisis sequence* → “Yalta” becomes, in ordinary speech, the portable word for any great-power carve-up done over the heads of the people living under it → the classrooms that never print “Yalta-Potsdam system” still live inside the consequences of those maps.
- **Lander.** “Another Yalta” is still a newspaper insult for any deal in which big powers draw lines on somebody else’s map. The reader has heard the city used as a verb.
- **Who tells it.** 2 of 11 print the system name (UA, BY); 4 of 11 run the arc without it (IT, UK, US, and RU’s settlement-only version); 0 of 11 tell the student that the label is itself a Cold War historiographical choice.
- **Receipts-lite.** Standard international-relations periodization (Yalta–Potsdam system / bipolarity) in any IR handbook; the conferences’ published protocols. <!-- CHECK: the label is common in post-Soviet historiography and less so in Anglophone school surveys -->
- **Why it fits.** Non-obvious, earned by a two-book signature the ladder already shows, shared lander (“Yalta” as carve-up word) across languages. Causal precision: the name did not create the zones — it is what two curricula need the student to treat as a complete world order before Korea opens. Strong braid into ch. 15 (the order’s UN wiring is the flag the next war is fought under).

### Pitch 4: The currency that closed the roads *(material — seed from US/IT/UA sequence)*

- **Seed (from the ladder).** OpenStax puts the Deutschemark into the blockade’s fuse: in June 1948 the UK and US introduce the new currency “into their zones as well as into the western portions of Berlin… Infuriated, the Soviet Union cut off all ground routes” (US p. 577). Italy stages the same causality as Western monetary reform and Marshall revival of a strong German state, then Stalin’s “trial of strength” (IT ~p. 504). Ukraine notes separate money reforms and a Soviet demand to fold western Berlin sectors into the Soviet zone’s financial system before the occupation administration “began a blockade” (UA p. 18). Belarus jumps to two German states without the siege. The comparison’s hinge: the freeze’s most famous crisis is also a currency fight.
- **Loop.** Four occupation powers cannot agree on one German money → the West launches a currency reform that makes West Berlin a shop window of recovery → Moscow answers by closing the ground routes that feed the shop window → the airlift turns a financial dispute into a year-long demonstration of which system can keep a city alive → the student who only remembers “blockade” never meets the banknote that started the siege.
- **Lander.** A banknote in the pocket is still the most ordinary object of sovereignty. Anyone who has changed money at a border already knows that currency lines are political lines; Berlin 1948 is that lesson at airlift scale. <!-- CHECK: West German Deutsche Mark introduced 20 June 1948; Soviet answer and blockade follow within days -->
- **Who tells it.** 3 of 11 put currency reform next to the blockade (US, IT, UA); 1 of 11 has the blockade without the money fuse in the quoted beat (UK stresses starvation aim); 1 of 11 skips the blockade (BY); 0 of 11 make the banknote the chapter’s lander.
- **Why it fits.** Material, checkable, slightly surprising (money before planes in the causal chain). Causal precision: the Deutschemark did not invent the Cold War — it is what turned an occupation quarrel into a siege the whole world could watch. Risk: denser economic detail than a tired adult may want after the ladder.
