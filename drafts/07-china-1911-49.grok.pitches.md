### Pitch 1: The army whose name is still a verdict *(linguistic lander — recommended)*

- **Seed (from the ladder).** CN alone titles the endgame 人民解放战争 — “People’s War of Liberation” — and OpenStax alone among the Western surveys puts the English calque into the victory sentence: “the CCP’s People’s Liberation Army decisively defeated the forces of the GMD” (US, p. 582). Everyone else on the ladder says “civil war” or “communists.” The noun travels; the verdict stays Chinese.
- **Loop.** 1946–49 is fought as a party war for the mainland → the winning side names both the campaign and the army as *liberation* (解放), not as a neutral civil conflict → after 1949 that army name becomes permanent state vocabulary — parades, white papers, Taiwan-strait headlines — while schoolbooks outside China keep teaching the same years as “Chinese civil war.”
- **Lander.** “PLA” is still the abbreviation on every major news wire when ships and aircraft move near Taiwan. A reader who only ever met “civil war” has been using one classroom’s noun for a fight another classroom still names as liberation.
- **Who tells it.** 1 of 11 (CN) uses People’s War of Liberation as lesson title and lead verb; 1 of 11 (US) uses People’s Liberation Army in the 1949 sentence; 0 of 11 join the war-name to the army-name still in global English.
- **Receipts-lite.** CN 纲要 上 Unit 8 / 第25课 title 人民解放战争; PLA as official English for 中国人民解放军; Taiwan-strait reporting conventions on major wires. <!-- CHECK: PLA official English name; 解放 as PRC period label for 1949 campaigns -->
- **Why it fits.** It is the chapter’s naming question turned into a living word — same axis as the ladder, shared across corpus countries via news English, braids forward to Korea (ch. 15) where CN again fights under a war-title-as-verdict (抗美援朝).

### Pitch 2: Who sat as “China” until 1971 *(map / news lander — feeds Korea)*

- **Seed (from the ladder).** OpenStax: the United States “maintained that Nationalist-led Taiwan was the ‘real’ China” (US, p. 582). Ukraine: “its place in the UN until 1971 was occupied by representatives of the Kuomintang” (UA, p. 125). Italy: the US continued to treat the Taiwan government as legitimate and that government held China’s UN seat until 1971 (IT, p. 516). Three books name the seat; none runs the consequence into the next war.
- **Loop.** October 1949 produces two claimants to one China → the UN seat and the “China” name in Western diplomacy stay with the Republic of China on Taiwan through the 1950s and 1960s → when the Korean War opens in 1950, the Security Council acts with the Soviet Union absent and with Beijing unseated, so the flag that votes is not the government that holds the mainland → only in 1971 does UN General Assembly Resolution 2758 seat the PRC. <!-- CHECK: UNGA Res. 2758, 25 Oct 1971; Soviet UNSC boycott Jan–Aug 1950 over PRC representation -->
- **Lander.** “China” on a UN nameplate is still a live argument in adult news (who represents the state; what Taiwan is called). The reader who follows Security Council stories is living inside a seating fight that began on this chapter’s last page.
- **Who tells it.** 3 of 11 hold pieces of the UN/Taiwan recognition story (US, UA, IT); 0 of 11 connect 1949 seating to the Korean War’s UN machinery (that join is general history for the thread, not a textbook quote).
- **Receipts-lite.** UNGA Res. 2758 (1971); Truman/US non-recognition of PRC until 1979; Korea ch. 15 empty-chair / UN-flag problem. <!-- CHECK: US recognized PRC 1 Jan 1979 -->
- **Why it fits.** Strongest braid to the finale; causal precision required — the empty chair did not cause the Korean War, it shaped which “China” and which UN the war was fought under (AGENT.md punchline rule).

### Pitch 3: The currency that died before the regime did *(material lander)*

- **Seed (from the ladder / adjacent pages).** CN’s Liberation lesson stages Nationalist economic collapse: unlimited paper issue, hyperinflation, prices soaring — “1948 年前 8 个月纸币的发行量已经是 1937 年全年发行量的 47 万倍” (note extract, civil-war economic crisis). <!-- CHECK: verify exact CN inflation sentence against printed p. ~164 --> IN notes prices rose ~30% per month 1945–1949 (dossier). US stresses GMD hyperinflation and alienation. The political defeat has a wallet.
- **Loop.** Wartime and civil-war finance prints money to pay armies → urban middle classes and wage earners watch savings evaporate in 1947–49 → the Nationalist state’s fiscal failure hollows the cities the PLA then takes → the PRC’s early money and price controls are sold as exit from that wreck, and a new currency order replaces the one that burned.
- **Lander.** Anyone who has held a banknote that lost value in a crisis, or who follows stories about inflation in the news, already owns the mechanism. The civil war was also a crash of paper.
- **Who tells it.** CN + fragments in IN/US; 0 of 11 make the inflation the hinge of why the KMT lost the cities.
- **Receipts-lite.** Gold yuan reform 1948; contemporary price series in Republican financial histories; CN textbook’s 470,000× note-issue claim needs PDF check. <!-- CHECK: Gold yuan introduced Aug 1948; collapse 1948–49 -->
- **Risk.** Strong material hook if the inflation claim verifies cleanly; weaker shared “object on the table” than tea or a phone — money is universal but the specific 1948 note is not in the reader’s pocket.

### Pitch 4: The Long March as a phrase you already use *(my candidate — non-obvious)*

- **Seed (from the ladder).** UK alone walks the march with survey arithmetic: “almost 100 000 communists set out… Eventually the 20 000 survivors found refuge at Yenan” (UK, pp. 425–426). Italy: ~100,000 leave, fewer than 10,000 arrive, 10,000 km (IT, p. 458). CN: Central Red Army “forced” to begin the Long March; after four blockades “from over 80,000… to over 30,000”; the march “ended in victory” and seeds “Long March spirit” (CN, ~pp. 150, 152). Russia refuses the epic: “the Soviet movement in China suffered a defeat” (RU, p. 121). Same trek; four moral weather systems.
- **Loop.** A fighting retreat of 1934–36 is later canonised in PRC education as the party’s origin ordeal → Western surveys import the proper name “Long March” with body counts and occasionally with debunking asides (Lowe cites Chang/Halliday) → the phrase detaches from the map and becomes global English for any grinding, multi-year political or institutional struggle (“a long march through the institutions,” corporate and campaign usage).
- **Lander.** “Long march” is already in the reader’s mouth as metaphor. Few who use it could place Yan’an or reconcile 20 000 survivors with 10 000 arrivals with “victory.”
- **Who tells it.** 4 of 11 narrate the march with any density (CN, UK, IT, RU); headcount spreads are UK/IT/CN only; 0 of 11 note the metaphor’s afterlife in English.
- **Receipts-lite.** Edgar Snow, *Red Star Over China* (1937) — the reportage Lowe quotes; Yan’an as CCP seat late 1930s; Oxford/Webster political usage of “long march.” <!-- CHECK: Snow 1937; metaphor diffusion mid/late 20th c. -->
- **Why it is mine.** It grows from the comparison’s number-and-verdict split (victory vs defeat vs body count) rather than from CN’s self-label alone, and the lander is linguistic-but-everyday without requiring Chinese news literacy.
