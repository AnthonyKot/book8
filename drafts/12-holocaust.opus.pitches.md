### Pitch 1: The bill for the glass

- **Seed (from the ladder).** Four of the six books call November 1938 by a name made of shop
  windows — *Kristallnacht* (UK), *Kristallnacht*, "the 'Night of Broken Glass'" (US),
  *notte dei cristalli* (IT), «Хрустальная ночь» (RU). Chubaryan alone prints the accounting that
  produced the name, and prints it in one breath with the arrests: «Ущерб только от разбитых
  витрин составил 5 млн марок. С этим связано другое название этого события — „Ночь разбитых
  витрин“. Более 20 тыс. евреев были брошены в концентрационные лагеря.» (RU, p. 93). The German
  booklet never uses the word: twice it says *der Pogrom 1938*.
- **Loop.** The plate glass was insured, and German insurers were liable → at a conference in the
  Reich Air Ministry on 12 November 1938 the liability was reversed: the insurance payouts were
  confiscated by the state, the Jewish community was ordered to pay for the repairs itself, and a
  collective "atonement payment" of one billion Reichsmarks was imposed on German Jews → part of
  the glass had to be re-imported from Belgium in scarce foreign currency, which is why the
  breakage was totted up in marks at all → the number that gave the night its name is a damage
  assessment, and the name is what four classrooms in this corpus still teach.
- **Lander (linguistic, shared).** The word itself. A reader in Moscow, Rome, London or Chicago
  learned this event under a name that counts the windows and not the people — and it is still
  the name in their newspaper. German official and scholarly usage moved to *Novemberpogrome*
  decades ago, for exactly that reason.
- **Who tells it.** 4 of 11 classrooms use the glass name; 1 of 11 (RU) prints the damage figure
  that the name is made of; 1 of 11 (DE) declines the name; 0 of 11 say who ended up paying the
  bill.
- **Receipts-lite.** The stenographic record of Göring's 12 November 1938 conference survives and
  was used at Nuremberg; the one-billion-Reichsmark levy on German Jews, decreed the same day; the
  Reichspogromnacht memorials on 9 November in most German cities.
  <!-- CHECK: Göring conference at the Reich Air Ministry, 12 Nov 1938, Nuremberg doc. PS-1816; Verordnung über eine Sühneleistung der Juden deutscher Staatsangehörigkeit, 12 Nov 1938, RM 1bn; insurance payments confiscated to the Reich; plate glass partly imported from Belgium and repaid in foreign exchange -->
- **Why it fits.** The chapter is already an argument about nouns — *Menschen* vs *евреев* at the
  ravine, "factories of death" vs the refusal of that metaphor. This adds the one noun the reader
  has said out loud themselves.
- **Risk.** The insurance detail is the kind of fact that reads as a gotcha if the sentences get
  clever. It has to be delivered as bookkeeping.

### Pitch 2: The man who had to invent a word *(my candidate — recommended; non-obvious, and braids to ch. 14)*

- **Seed (from the comparison, and from a silence).** Belarus's world history never names a Jewish
  victim and never uses the word "Holocaust," but its glossary of historical concepts defines
  «Геноци́д (от греч. genos — род, племя и лат. caedo — убиваю)» — a word with an etymology and no
  event attached. Italy spends a page arguing the word is used improperly ("appare improprio usare
  il termine 'genocidio'", IT p. 480). Russia applies it to the Slavic peoples in the same
  paragraph as the Holocaust (RU p. 192). Three classrooms handle the word very differently and
  not one of them says where it came from.
- **Loop.** The word did not exist before 1944. Raphael Lemkin, a Polish Jewish lawyer who had been
  arguing since 1933 that international law had no name for the destruction of a group, escaped to
  the United States and built the term in *Axis Rule in Occupied Europe* — Greek *genos*, Latin
  *caedere*, the exact two roots the Belarusian glossary prints → he wrote it as a description of
  German occupation policy while his own family was being killed; 49 of his relatives died → he
  then spent 1946–48 lobbying delegation by delegation at the new United Nations until the Genocide
  Convention was adopted on 9 December 1948, the day before the Universal Declaration of Human
  Rights → every classroom in this corpus that uses the word, including the one that never attaches
  it to anybody, is using a word one survivor manufactured to describe this.
- **Lander (linguistic, shared everywhere).** "Genocide" is in every reader's news feed and in most
  of their political arguments. It is roughly eighty years old, it has an author, and it was built
  for this event. That is checkable in two minutes and almost nobody has been told it.
- **Who tells it.** 0 of 11 classrooms name Lemkin (grep-verified: zero hits for Lemkin / Лемкин /
  Лемкін across all eleven originals and all eleven English discovery files). 1 of 11 prints his
  etymology without him (BY). 1 of 11 argues about the word's proper use (IT). 0 of 11 date the
  word.
- **Receipts-lite.** Raphael Lemkin, *Axis Rule in Occupied Europe* (Carnegie Endowment, 1944),
  ch. IX; UN General Assembly resolution 260 (III), 9 December 1948; Lemkin's papers at the
  American Jewish Historical Society.
  <!-- CHECK: Lemkin coined "genocide" in Axis Rule in Occupied Europe, 1944; Genocide Convention adopted 9 Dec 1948, entered into force 12 Jan 1951; the figure of 49 murdered relatives is Lemkin's own -->
- **Why it fits.** It answers the question the chapter's ladder leaves standing — how do you name a
  thing nobody had a name for — with a dated object rather than a moral. It also hands ch. 14 its
  first paragraph: the 1945–49 order includes a convention, and the convention exists because of a
  word invented in 1944.
- **Causal precision guard.** Lemkin's word did not produce the convention on its own and the
  convention did not stop anything. What the word produced is narrower and true: a legal category
  that did not exist in 1939, under which acts could afterwards be charged. Say that, and stop.

### Pitch 3: Why three books almost agree about a ravine

- **Seed (from the ladder).** UK: "some 34 000 Jews were murdered in a ravine at Babi Yar." DE:
  "more than 33,000 people were shot by SS and police," in two days. RU: "about 70 thousand Jews
  were shot," undated. The chapter already refuses to reconcile these.
- **Loop.** The near-identical 33–34,000 is not independent corroboration: it is one document
  reappearing. The Einsatzgruppen filed daily reports that were compiled in Berlin and circulated
  as numbered bulletins; report no. 101 records 33,771 shot at Babi Yar on 29–30 September 1941 →
  copies survived the war in the security-police files and became evidence at Nuremberg, which is
  why two classrooms in different countries print the same figure → the same apparatus that
  produced the count also tried to erase it: in 1943 a prisoner unit was made to reopen the ravine
  and burn the bodies before the Red Army arrived → so the precise number and the missing number
  have the same author, and the higher Soviet figure is measuring the site over two years rather
  than the weekend the report covers.
- **Lander (material, shared).** Every casualty figure the reader has ever read from that war —
  in any of these eleven books — reaches them through somebody's filing system. At Babi Yar the
  filing system belonged to the killers. It is the reason the number is exact to three digits.
- **Who tells it.** 3 of 11 classrooms give a Babi Yar number; 2 of 3 date it to two days; 0 of 11
  say where the number came from; 0 of 11 mention the 1943 exhumation.
- **Receipts-lite.** Ereignismeldung UdSSR Nr. 101 (Reich Security Main Office, Berlin);
  Sonderkommando 1005; the Babyn Yar site in Kyiv, and the successive inscriptions on its
  monuments.
  <!-- CHECK: Ereignismeldung UdSSR Nr. 101 records 33,771 for 29–30 Sept 1941; ~195 Ereignismeldungen compiled Jun 1941–Apr 1942; Sonderkommando 1005 exhumations at Babi Yar, Aug–Sept 1943; the 1976 Soviet monument's inscription names "Soviet citizens" and not Jews -->
- **Why it fits.** It is the chapter's own numbers fight, resolved without picking a winner — the
  outcome is a fact about provenance, not an accusation.
- **Risk.** Highest of the four for register drift, and the Soviet-monument detail belongs in the
  thread only if it is stated as an inscription, not as an indictment.

### Pitch 4: Four Pfennig per person per kilometre

- **Seed (from the ladder).** Four books describe the transport and none of them describes it as a
  transaction. UK: a conference "to discuss the logistics of how to remove up to 11 million Jews
  from their homes in all parts of Europe and transport them into the occupied territories."
  US: "railroad lines were built specifically to transport prisoners to them." DE: "from July 1942
  trains with deported Jews from all of Western Europe arrived regularly." IT: "then they were
  deported to prison camps (lager)."
- **Loop.** The German state railway was a business and billed for the work: the SS was charged the
  standard third-class group-excursion tariff, roughly four Pfennig per person per track
  kilometre, with children at half and the youngest free; the empty return run was billed too;
  bookings ran through a travel bureau → the invoices had to be paid, so the deportations were
  funded out of property confiscated from the deported → which meant the transport had to be
  scheduled against ordinary timetables, in competition with military traffic, by clerks who saw
  only passenger numbers and kilometres.
- **Lander (material, shared across every corpus country).** A fare per kilometre with a child
  discount — the same pricing logic on the reader's own train ticket. The tariff is the ordinary
  thing; what it was applied to is not.
- **Who tells it.** 4 of 11 classrooms describe the transport; 1 of 11 (US) says the lines were
  built for it; 0 of 11 say who was billed or who paid.
- **Receipts-lite.** Raul Hilberg, "German Railroads / Jewish Souls" (1976), the standard account,
  built on surviving Reichsbahn correspondence; Deutsche Bahn's own "Sonderzüge in den Tod"
  exhibition, first shown in German stations from 2008.
  <!-- CHECK: Reichsbahn group-excursion rate c. 4 Pf per person per km, half fare under 10, free under 4, group rate from 400 passengers; empty return charged; bookings via Mitteleuropäisches Reisebüro; DB "Sonderzüge in den Tod" exhibition from 2008 -->
- **Why it fits.** It is the strongest answer in the set to the chapter's actual question — how many
  people are in the sentence — because it adds a category of participant no book on the ladder
  names: the ones who were doing paperwork.
- **Risk.** The pricing detail is vivid enough to become the point. It must stay a tariff.

it survives the register rule without effort because the story is bibliographic rather than
