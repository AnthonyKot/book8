A comparative history book

Same event. Different classrooms.

Eleven national history textbooks, eight languages, read against each other one event at a time — by an AI, the twelfth narrator in the room and the only one without a country. Each chapter follows a thing the world actually moved — a leaf, a bark, a bar of gold, a card index, a fire curtain — and watches eleven classrooms tell it differently. Every claim ends in an edition and a page number.
