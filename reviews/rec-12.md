# Recommendations — Chapter 12, “The Holocaust”

1. **Make the lede collect the occupation that Chapter 11 has just created.** Keep the Wannsee scene, but precede it with a sentence in substance: **“The previous chapter ended with Poland divided between two occupying armies; in German-occupied Poland, exclusion became confinement, forced labour, shooting and deportation.”** Then add, immediately before the fifteen men sit down, that mass murder was already under way by January 1942. This both carries the reader across the chapter break and prevents Wannsee from reading as the meeting where the Holocaust began.

2. **Put a human being into the opening before the chapter turns people into totals.** The current lede gives us fifteen officials, thirty copies and ninety minutes; the first image is a tabulating machine. Introduce one fully sourced Jewish person caught in the policy—a name, place and surviving trace—from the existing research, in two or three sentences, then return to the minutes. Do not make that person representative of six million. The point is to stop a chapter about the disappearance of people from making perpetrators and paperwork its only visible lives.

3. **Rename “The boring facts” and cut the repeated house instruction.** Use **“What happened”** or **“The fixed points,”** and delete “Dry scaffolding. These lines are the ruler; the ladder is the measurement.” Chapter 11 has just used virtually the same sentence and Chapter 13 will do it again; this is precisely where the book’s template begins to sound indifferent to its subject.

4. **Stop presenting the ladder as a clean ranking from “thinnest” to “thickest” agency.** The Russian sentence, in which “a targeted policy” acts, is less agentive than the Ukrainian phrase “at the hands of the Nazis,” while the British row names one principal actor but also stages a historiographical argument. Keep every blockquote intact, but replace the single ascent with explicit comparison tags: **named decision-maker / named killing units / wider collaborators / passive or abstract action.** If the table must remain ordered, group the rows as **abstract policy, concentrated responsibility, distributed responsibility** rather than pretending that six unlike passages occupy exact rungs.

5. **Rewrite the opening of “The tell” so it does not overclaim unanimity.** Replace “Nothing on this ladder is in dispute” and “none of them hedges” with the narrower finding the exhibit supports: **all six identify the systematic murder of European Jews and put the Jewish death toll at roughly five to six million; they differ over who enters the sentence as an actor.** The chapter itself later shows disagreements over Babi Yar’s count, the boundaries of victim categories and whether exact camp totals can be known.

6. **Turn the Hitler count into one sharp comparison, not a roll call.** Keep “forty times” in Lowe, “not once” in OpenStax and “sixteen times” in the German booklet; move the fifteen-name German list to receipts. Then rewrite the next paragraph around the interpretive result: Lowe concentrates causation at the top, OpenStax describes operating machinery without naming its head, and the German booklet holds leadership and distributed participation in the same frame. The current list of Globocnik, Jeckeln, Greiser, Stuckart, Freisler and the rest makes the reader count names instead of grasping the structure.

7. **Cut the three-paragraph Babi Yar number investigation down to one agency paragraph.** Retain the intact textbook sentences and the contrast that the German account says “SS and police shoot” where the Russian account says Jews “were shot.” Move the two-day-versus-whole-site reconciliation, the 33,771 operational-report figure and the caution about Chubaryan’s 70,000 to “Where this stops being true.” The facts section already gives Babi Yar two bullets; the present tell visits the ravine three more times and temporarily turns an agency exhibit into a casualty-accounting essay.

8. **Remove the November 1938 naming digression from the main narrative.** Move the Russian “Night of Broken Shop Windows” pull quote intact, plus one concise note on the German booklet’s use of “pogrom,” to receipts or a terminology note. It is a strong comparison for a chapter about euphemism, but this chapter asks “Who murdered?” and the passage introduces a second question—what the event is called—just before a third question arrives with the “death factory” metaphor. Keep the factory passage: its warning that a machine has no hands speaks directly to the chapter’s governing problem and leads naturally into the paperwork thread.

9. **Make “The death factory” the hinge into the thread.** Move the German pull quote and the sentence “The other refuses the machine because a machine has no hands in it” to the end of the tell, then open the thread with the concrete Berlin card-index example now buried in its fourth paragraph. A clean sequence is: **metaphor can hide hands → an office had cards and handed names to the Gestapo → continental totals depended on administrative records.** That produces narrative propulsion instead of restarting with another three-textbook inventory.

10. **Narrow “The thread · where the number came from” to what the evidence can safely bear.** Cut the corporate chronology from “majority-owned by IBM from 1922” through postwar recovery, and move the Edwin Black controversy paragraph to limits or receipts. Rewrite “What is not in dispute is … who owned them” as a precise account of what the cited evidence establishes: which censuses gathered which categories, where Hollerith systems were documented, and what cannot be claimed about their necessity or operational role. The current version walks the reader toward an IBM revelation, retreats from the strongest version, and leaves the emotional emphasis on corporate ownership rather than the many offices and choices the chapter set out to expose.

11. **Give the thread a present tense more exact than “the punched card is an ancestor of the database record.”** End it with the continuing administrative act, not a technology genealogy: forms still choose categories, institutions still join records, and a person can still become visible to power through data collected for an apparently ordinary purpose. Phrase this as a capacity and a civic question, not a claim that modern databases lead to mass murder. Then compress “Who teaches the paperwork” to its real finding—none of the eleven books explains the administrative layer, while the German booklet comes closest—without repeating every zero search term in the narrative.

12. **Repair or replace both documentary images.** The tabulating-machine image appears before the reader knows why it matters and visually prejudges the chapter toward the IBM thread; replace it with the Wannsee country list or another document that makes the proposed eleven million visible, reserving the machine for the thread if it remains. Fix the second caption, which currently reads “Unknown authorUnknown author (Franz Konrad confess,” and give the Warsaw photograph a human caption explaining what the reader is seeing. Neither asset-database debris nor an unexplained machine belongs in so grave an opening.

13. **Compress “Credit where due” and “Where this stops being true” by at least half.** Credit bpb for distributed agency, Sabbatucci–Vidotto for the naming problem, and Lowe/OpenStax in one paired sentence for rival pedagogical virtues; all four cases have already been argued in the tell. In limits, retain the German genre warning, the Belarus and India syllabus cautions, the Babi Yar counting-window problem and the final admission that these are not lived histories. Move Kazakhstan, Sudan, OCR workflow, search stems and extraction cautions into receipts. After Chapter 11’s equally long credit-and-limits ceremony, this repetition drains force just before Chapter 13 starts the apparatus again.

14. **Let the revised paperwork thread close the chapter and write a careful handoff to Hiroshima.** Move the compressed credit, limits and receipts before the thread. After the thread’s final present-day turn, add a bridge in substance: **“The next chapter changes theatre and question: from an extermination policy carried through many offices and hands to a named wartime decision whose necessity and purpose remain disputed.”** This marks the moral and historical distinction rather than using “mass killing” as a loose connector. Then retain the required final line exactly: “Now look up what your textbook calls it.” Also update the target chapter’s “Feeds” line to include the immediate next chapter, not only Chapter 14.

**Verdict:** This chapter is an exhibit crowded by three rival essays; making the widening circle of agency its single spine would help it most.
