1. **Retitle the chapter.** “Pacific & Hiroshima” promises a Pacific-war comparison that never arrives; after the facts, the chapter is almost entirely about the atomic bomb’s agency, purpose, casualties, and afterlife as a unit. Change the heading to something that names the actual argument—“What Hiroshima Is For” is the cleanest option—and let the Pacific campaign remain essential context in the facts. If “Pacific” must stay in the title, then the ladder needs actual comparison of how the books narrate the Pacific war, not merely August 1945.

2. **Rewrite the lede as a deliberate handoff from Chapter 12.** The current opening—“At 8:15 in the morning on 6 August 1945…”—is precise but gives the reader another date-and-clock reset immediately after the Wannsee date-and-room opening. Begin instead with the conceptual turn: “The previous chapter ended with people made into countable rows. This one begins with a city becoming a number.” Then give the August chronology in two sentences and retain “What each of them needs the bomb to *do*…” as the destination. This makes the repetition of counting across the two chapters feel designed rather than accidental.

3. **Choose one governing measurement and make the question, ladder, and tell agree.** The question promises “space” (“how many lines”), the ladder says it is ordered by “agency,” and the second column asks “What the bomb is for.” Those are three different comparisons. Make purpose the spine: rewrite the question as “What does your textbook need the bomb to explain—surrender, Soviet power, civilian destruction, or nothing at all?” Order the ladder by that progression, and keep page-space as supporting evidence in the tell. “Thinnest agency” is too abstract to carry this chapter and repeats Chapter 12’s agency ladder.

4. **Compress “The boring facts” from eight bullets to five without dropping facts.** Merge the China/Pearl Harbor and island-campaign bullets into one Pacific-war setup; merge Tokyo and Trinity into one “before August” bullet; keep Potsdam; keep the 6–15 August sequence; merge casualty ranges and the unsettled necessity/Soviet-entry debate into the final bullet. Three consecutive chapters each presenting an eight-item scaffold makes the apparatus audible, and here the reader needs to reach the excellent comparison faster.

5. **Break the eight-row ladder into two readable movements.** Keep every retained textbook quotation verbatim and intact, but divide the table after Russia: “The bomb as omitted or compressed” (Ukraine through Russia), then “The bomb as a decision that must be argued” (Italy, Britain, United States). Put a one-sentence hinge between them. The current uninterrupted table is the chapter’s longest dead zone, and the final three substantial quotations are precisely where a pleasure reader is most likely to start skimming.

6. **Rebuild “The tell” by grammar and function, not as a second country-by-country parade.** The chapter currently walks Ukraine → India → China → Belarus → Russia → Italy/UK → OpenStax immediately after the ladder walked the same route. Merge the first five into two comparative passages: omission/passive construction (Ukraine and India), then compressed causal chains (China, Belarus, Russia). Give Italy, Lowe, and OpenStax one passage on the decision becoming contestable. Preserve the specific linguistic findings, but stop reintroducing every national column as though the table had not just been read. Chapter 12 uses the same table-then-roll-call motion, so this is where serial fatigue sets in.

7. **Move the China paragraph and Taiwan pull quote to the end of “The tell.”** The line “The paragraph is not going to Hiroshima; it is going to a date six weeks later” is the best doorway into Chapter 14’s postwar order, but it currently appears near the start and is abandoned. End the section with the intact Taiwan quotation and rewrite the commentary to land on the handoff: for this classroom, the atomic bombs are not the climax of a world war but one clause on the way to the national order that follows. That gives the book’s thematic—not strictly chronological—sequence a felt logic.

8. **Fix the contradiction at the opening of “The thread.”** It says, “The one book in this corpus that does not narrate Hiroshima as an event uses it as a unit,” while the ladder introduction says Kazakhstan “covers the bombings at length,” and the limits say it treats Hiroshima and Nagasaki inside the Semipalatinsk story. Rewrite the thread opening as: “The one book kept outside the ladder arrives at Hiroshima from a test site two thousand kilometres away.” Then move the Karaul quotation intact beneath it. The route into Hiroshima, not a questionable claim of non-narration, is the discovery.

9. **Cut the thread from five explanatory passes to three.** The chapter tells us repeatedly that Hiroshima becomes a quantity: “A city has become a quantity,” then the newsfeed paragraph, then “the unit has no agreed value,” then the final corpus count. Merge the first two paragraphs around the contrast between incomprehensible kilotons and a recognisable city; merge the last two around the unstable denominator; end on the excellent sentence “The multiplier is announced to three significant figures; the thing being multiplied varies by nearly half.” Move “Two of eleven classrooms…” to the receipts or cut it. That ending has bite; the current extra accounting talks past it.

10. **Verify or narrow the absolute claim “It is the only unit of measurement in ordinary use named after a place where people died.”** As written, it invites the reader to stop and hunt for counterexamples, and “unit of measurement” is being used metaphorically rather than technically. Rewrite it as a defensible claim about this usage—for example, that Hiroshima has become an unusually durable popular unit for explosive force—or supply evidence for the absolute. This is not a place to trade factual confidence for a grand sentence.

11. **Reduce “Credit where due” to one award and stop replaying the tell.** Keep OpenStax as the sole credit paragraph because “the narrating state’s own classroom describing its own act” produces the chapter’s sharpest reversal of expectation. Cut the Lowe and Chubaryan commendations here; their open verdict and casualty numbers have already been fully explained, and Chapter 12 has just praised the same two books for nearly the same virtues while Chapter 14 will praise Lowe and OpenStax again. Three consecutive ceremonies for the recurring winners make the fairness apparatus feel predetermined.

12. **Collapse “Where this stops being true” into three compact paragraphs.** Paragraph one: corpus and syllabus boundaries, including Ukraine and the non-peer status of Kazakhstan. Paragraph two: casualty windows and the two textbook errors. Paragraph three: extraction/translation cautions and the reminder that these are not Pacific military histories. Cut the repeated account of Kazakhstan’s Hiroshima-as-unit treatment, already the whole thread, and do not enumerate all five casualty totals a third time. The current six-paragraph audit drains the force of the essay just before the receipts.

13. **Move or replace the Enola Gay museum photograph.** Placed after the Semipalatinsk thread, it snaps the chapter back to American hardware just when the argument has travelled outward to test sites, arsenals, and the reader’s newsfeed. Move it beside the facts or the OpenStax material. In its present position, use an image of Semipalatinsk/Karaul or a visual record of yield comparison instead, so the second image advances the chapter’s distinctive essay rather than merely illustrating the bomb carrier.

14. **Add a two-sentence forward turn before the receipts, while preserving the required final line exactly.** The chapter should explicitly release the reader into “The 1945–1949 order”: the bomb was used to end a war in several of these accounts, but it also became a message to the Soviet Union and then a unit for the arms race. Put that turn at the end of the thread or limits, not after the receipts. Leave “Now look up what your textbook calls it.” unchanged as the closing line.

**Verdict:** This is an essay trapped inside an exhibit; making “a city becomes a unit” the chapter’s spine would help it most.
