# Recommendations — Chapter 1, “Opium Wars”

1. **Restore the missing “The thread” section and make it the chapter’s last narrative movement.** Build it from NCERT’s triangular trade: move from Chinese tea, to the East India Company’s attempt to transplant Chinese tea plants and expertise to British India, to the cup the reader can make now. Fact-check the Robert Fortune/Assam distinction so the chapter does not imply that Indian tea began with Fortune. End on the precise imperial habit Chapter 2 needs: treating a plant under someone else’s sovereignty as a supply problem. Chapter 2 currently says, “Chapter 1 ends with tea taken out of China and replanted in British India”; without this section, the book breaks its own handshake on the first page-turn.

2. **Cut the reader-facing line “Inherits: — · Feeds: Scramble for Africa (ch. 2), China 1911→49 (ch. 7).”** Move that information to the contents page or editorial metadata. “Inherits: —” makes the book open like a project dashboard, and announcing future dependencies is weaker than making the tea thread perform the transition.

3. **Rewrite the lede to put named people, contraband, and consequence into one continuous action.** For example: “On a beach at Humen in 1839, Lin Zexu’s officials destroy more than twenty thousand chests of smuggled opium surrendered by British and American merchants. Within a year, British warships are off China’s coast. By 1842, Hong Kong Island has been ceded and five treaty ports opened. The textbooks agree on that sequence. They do not agree on which act made it a war.” This preserves the facts but replaces “are in the approaches” and the workshop sentence “What each classroom does…” with an actual invitation.

4. **Replace “Who started it — and what word does your textbook need for the side that moved first?” with a question the ladder literally answers:** “Where does the story begin: with the opium trade, the Chinese seizure, or the British decision to use force?” Chapter 2 asks another agency-and-grammar question; narrowing Chapter 1 to the choice of starting point prevents two consecutive chapters from feeling like the same demonstration with different nouns.

5. **Rename “The boring facts” as “What happened” and cut “Dry scaffolding. These lines are the ruler; the ladder is the measurement.”** The exact wording recurs in Chapter 2, so a reader meets house boilerplate twice before the book has earned it. More importantly, the first chapter should not instruct a pleasure reader that the next material is boring.

6. **Resolve the plural-title mismatch.** The ladder and almost all of “The tell” analyze how the **First** Opium War begins; the Second War receives one baseline bullet and one aside about Sabbatucci–Vidotto’s label. Retitle the chapter “The First Opium War,” while retaining the Second War in the factual aftermath, or add a distinct paragraph in “The tell” that genuinely compares how the books name and explain the second conflict. Do not let “Opium Wars” promise a two-war comparison the chapter does not deliver.

7. **Move the final “boring facts” bullet out of the factual baseline:** “Later Chinese historiography often folds the wars into a longer ‘century of humiliation.’ Western school surveys often treat them as the opening of China to Western commerce.” Put it in “The tell,” narrow it to “In this set,” and attach it to the Chinese/UK contrast already demonstrated there. As written, the ruler announces the result before the ladder measures it and makes a broader East/West claim than the six editions can support.

8. **Rebuild “The ladder” for vertical reading without touching any quotation.** Remove the separate “One-line tag” column and place each tag beneath its classroom label; keep every blockquote verbatim. A three-column table turns the chapter’s main pleasure—the voices—into a wide comparison grid, while the tags (“Victory as arrival,” “Colonial checklist,” and so on) compete with the prose interpretation that immediately follows.

9. **Rewrite “Belarus keeps the scare quotes on «опиумной»” as “Belarus prints ‘Opium’ in quotation marks” unless the source typography independently proves a distancing gesture.** The chapter is strongest when it demonstrates agency from syntax; assigning intent to punctuation gives a skeptical reader an avoidable reason to distrust the larger grammatical reading.

10. **Cut the standalone three-paragraph “Credit where due” section and merge its one new judgment into the final NCERT paragraph of “The tell.”** The section repeats, in order, that NCERT supplies India and the trade triangle, OpenStax gives the clearest sequence, and the Chinese book supplies names and depth—all points made only a few paragraphs earlier. Keep a single closing sentence naming NCERT as the chapter’s most useful triangulation if the credit ritual must survive; do not make the reader take the same lap twice. Chapter 2 repeats the credit apparatus, so redundancy here teaches fatigue before the second chapter begins.

11. **Separate reader-facing limits from production notes.** In “Where this stops being true,” replace “six books that bother with the 1840s” with “six books whose syllabus reaches the 1840s,” then keep the syllabus and military-history limits in the prose. Move “Machine-translated discovery files led us to the pages” and “Before any of them becomes a hard claim in a later rewrite…” into “Receipts” or an editor’s note. That workflow language makes a finished chapter sound unverified and provisional just before its close.

12. **Let the chapter finish on discovery, not compliance.** Use the order facts → ladder → tell → compressed credit/limits → receipts link → tea thread → “Now look up what your textbook calls it.” The current run of credit, limits, and bibliography drains the energy from the India paragraph; ending on the restored tea story gives Chapter 1 an essay’s afterimage and hands Chapter 2 its cinchona connection without explanatory throat-clearing.

**Verdict:** This chapter is currently a sharp exhibit; the single change that would help it most is restoring the tea-transplant thread and letting that living connection, not the apparatus, carry the reader into Chapter 2.
