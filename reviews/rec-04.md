# Recommendations for Chapter 4, “Who started 1914”

1. **Rewrite the lede so its claim matches the corpus and its accident does not masquerade as an explanation.** “Every book in this chapter prints the same calendar” is contradicted by the chapter itself: only five narrate the crisis, three in-scope books do not open it, and the five disagree over a consequential date. Keep the car and pistol, then pivot harder: “That accident explains the murder. It does not explain the next thirty-eight days. Five textbooks narrate those days; three begin the war somewhere else; even the five cannot agree which move made war.”

2. **Pay off Chapter 3 immediately, then stop retelling it.** A reader arrives from a chapter whose thread promises that the 1905 naval reset and arms race lead here. Move the useful core of “One thread does run back through this book’s own Part I” to a two-sentence bridge just after the lede or question: the Schlieffen Plan was dated to 1905 and assumed Russian slowness after defeat by Japan. Cut the rest of that paragraph, especially “The chapter before this one is about that defeat”; the reader has just read it.

3. **Make the mobilisation-date discrepancy the spine of “The tell,” not its eighth paragraph.** The passage beginning “One date splits the corpus” is the chapter’s cleanest exhibit: one compressed date changes who appears to move first. Put it directly after “Start with what nobody disputes,” set the five versions in a compact date strip or four-row comparison, and let the later grammar readings answer the question it raises. This would turn the section from five sequential book reports into an escalating argument.

4. **Rewrite the ladder’s axis in terms a reader can actually test.** “Thinnest agency” to “thickest” does not quite explain why the mixed Belarusian and Russian verdicts occupy their respective rungs, or why Lowe’s survey of competing theories is automatically “thickest.” Name the progression precisely—something like “war as automatic mechanism → shared culpability → a chosen first mover → deliberate provocation”—and make every one-line tag use that same causal vocabulary.

5. **Compress the first half of “The tell” by comparing sentences across books instead of walking the ladder a second time.** Merge the OpenStax, Belarus, Russia, and Italy paragraphs into two comparative movements: first the passive machinery (“pulled,” “caused,” “brought”), then the small verdict-bearing words (“only,” “however,” “unprovoked,” “first”). Preserve every textbook quotation intact, but cut the repeated plot summaries already visible in the table. Lowe can then stand alone because his historiographical lesson is genuinely a different kind of evidence.

6. **Cut the speculative national mind-reading.** Delete “it costs Italy nothing, because Italy is not in the July Crisis” and “A market textbook in the country with the most to lose from that theory.” Neither claim is demonstrated, and both reduce textual choices to national self-interest just when the chapter is strongest at showing internal contradiction. Also change “A fourteen-year-old is being handed a bibliography” to “A student”; the edition does not establish that exact reader.

7. **Keep the non-European counterframe, but stop calling omission a deliberate refusal.** “What they are declining is … the premise that a European quarrel is where the world’s history starts” conflicts with the later, more careful limit that these books use a different unit of analysis. Rewrite it as: “Their unit of analysis begins elsewhere: with what the European war made possible in Shandong, Sudan, or the wider colonial world.” Compress the Cairo analogy to two sentences and cut the Kosovo sentence; the anniversary echo is clever but sends the reader into a second assassination story without strengthening the inference.

8. **Turn toward the passport sooner in “The thread,” and state that this is an afterlife of war, not a missed chance to stop Princip.** The opening currently spends a quotation and a long paragraph on the conspirators before revealing the modern object. After the intact Italian quotation, add the destination plainly: “The lasting border change was not that these men escaped a passport check. It was that, within weeks, ordinary travellers everywhere became security subjects.” Then move quickly to 5 August 1914.

9. **Qualify the overbroad border aphorism.** Replace “A frontier was a customs post. It taxed what crossed. It did not ask who did” with a version bounded by the evidence: “Across much of western and central Europe, customs officers routinely policed goods while ordinary travellers often crossed without passports.” The present absolute is punchier than the facts support, especially after the paragraph has acknowledged Russian and Ottoman exceptions and a clandestine smuggling route.

10. **Recast the passport thread around state legibility, because Chapter 5 already owns the ‘temporary measure becomes permanent standard’ essay.** Keep the vivid 1915 photograph and facial-description table, but trim the 1920 booklet dimensions and the institutional genealogy. Promote “A person had become a set of readable fields” from a paragraph ending into the controlling line. This makes Chapter 4 about the new readable citizen, while Versailles can remain about institutions and standards that outlive their settlement.

11. **Replace the thread’s audit-style ending with a human circular image.** Cut “0 of 11 classrooms connect…” and “There was nothing yet to remark on, which is the whole of the finding”; after several earlier counts, this sounds like a research memo and makes absence do more work than it can. End by returning to the crossing: “The conspirators crossed with weapons hidden in their bags. A year later, the ordinary traveller crossed with a photograph and a table describing his face.” Preserve the current airport queue if desired, but let the person—not the corpus count—have the last beat.

12. **Collapse “Credit where due” into a short box rather than repeating the tell three times.** Lowe’s open argument, the Italian refusal of inevitability, and Chubaryan’s admission of Russian war aims have all just received full paragraphs. Keep one sentence of credit for each, or retain only the genuinely additive Chubaryan point about Russia’s own aims. At present this section makes a reader feel the recurring apparatus more than the chapter’s discovery.

13. **Strip production notes out of “Where this stops being true.”** Keep the first paragraph about the thin five-of-eight base and the final paragraph about the documentary scale. Move the “both” typo, the Italian date error, Fischer’s translation date, and the Arabic extraction warning into the receipts or an editorial note. Coming immediately before receipts, four paragraphs of textual housekeeping stall a chapter that should be accelerating toward its close; the same Arabic-extraction warning also returns in Chapter 5.

14. **Build the handoff to Versailles around the repeated date.** Near the end of “The tell,” after responsibility has been established as the chapter’s live dispute, add: “Five years to the day after the Sarajevo killings, two German ministers signed at Versailles, and the argument about responsibility became treaty language.” Keep the full Article 231 fact in “The boring facts.” This gives the next chapter a dramatic threshold instead of merely a navigation link.

**Verdict:** This is an exhibit with a strong essay attached; rebuilding “The tell” around the disputed mobilisation date would help it most.
