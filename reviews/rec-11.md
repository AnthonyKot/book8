# Recommendations — Chapter 11, “Munich → Pact → September 1939”

1. **Make the “wiring diagrams” the chapter’s governing comparison, not a late recap.** Move “**The wiring diagrams.** Same nodes — Versailles, Munich, collective security, the Pact, 1 September, 17 September. Only the arrows move” to immediately after the facts and turn the six bullets into a compact six-row table with columns for **Munich / Pact / September**. That is the comparison promised by the lede and question box. At present the formal ladder arrives halfway through and measures agency on 17 September, while the chapter advertises causality; the apparatus and the argument are about different things.

2. **Demote the present “Act III · The ladder” to a close-up within that larger comparison.** Retitle it **“Close-up: who crosses Poland’s border on 17 September?”** and keep all six textbook quotations intact, but stop asking this one morning to carry the whole chapter’s ladder. Its verb analysis is strong evidence for one arrow in the wiring diagram; it is not by itself an answer to “What caused the war?”

3. **Cut the two consecutive country parades in Acts I and II by at least a third.** Munich currently proceeds UK → US → IT → DE → RU → BY; the Pact then proceeds DE → RU → BY → UK → IT → a three-country protocol correction; the tell tours the same countries again. Rebuild those sections around disputed links—**what Munich cost, what Moscow inferred, what the Pact enabled**—and place countries against one another inside each paragraph. Move every retained textbook quotation whole into the new comparison or pull boxes; cut the surrounding résumé prose that restates it. A reader coming from Chapter 10 has already endured a long six-country rung-by-rung replay.

4. **Correct the lede’s chronology and withdraw its claim of perfect unanimity.** “Two armies enter Poland from opposite directions, **three weeks apart**” should say **sixteen days apart**. Replace “Every textbook in this chapter has the same dates” with something like **“The six accounts share the sequence; even where a date slips, their arrows do not.”** The limits later disclose Lowe’s defensible 24 August dating and OpenStax’s approximate “about two weeks,” so the present boast is both factually loose and needlessly vulnerable.

5. **Use the lede to collect Chapter 10 rather than starting another sealed file.** After the paper held over Chamberlain’s head, add one sentence connecting Spain’s broken Non-Intervention Agreement to the new chapter’s succession of agreements: governments have just watched a signed restraint fail in practice; now they try to purchase peace with another. Keep the causal wording exact—Spain did not “cause Munich”—but make the immediate reading sequence felt. The metadata says “Inherits: Versailles” and ignores the chapter the reader actually finished.

6. **Rename “The boring facts” and drop the house instruction.** Use **“What happened”** or **“The fixed points”** and cut “Dry scaffolding. These lines are the ruler; the ladder is the measurement.” Chapters 10 and 12 use the same language. By the third consecutive occurrence, the apparatus is announcing itself more loudly than the history, and calling the facts boring is a poor bargain with a pleasure reader.

7. **Rewrite the Belarus analysis so it says only what the quotation supports.** “In the Belarusian row nothing crosses anything” is fair; “a complete, self-consistent sentence about an event with no one in it” is not. The sacred quotation explicitly names the USSR taking a population “under its protection” and the Red Army’s campaign. Say instead: **the actors are named, but the border crossing, order-giver and Polish territory disappear; agency is redirected from entry into Poland to protection and reunification.** That is sharper because it does not overstate the grammatical trick.

8. **Delete the label “the three market books.”** Replace “Then the three market books, in ascending order of exposure” with **“The British, Italian and American accounts name progressively more of the act.”** OpenStax is not usefully classified as a “market book,” and the unexplained category sounds like an ideological grouping the chapter has neither defined nor demonstrated.

9. **Cut the NCERT “to Poland us” paragraph from the tell and move it intact to receipts, if it must remain.** The paragraph beginning “One book in the corpus draws no arrow here at all” jumps back to Chapter 1, eighteenth-century partitions and Chinese reformers after the six wiring diagrams have finally landed. It is a terrific marginal curiosity and a bad ending to the central analysis. End instead on “Two have it pointing forward, into Poland. All six draw it.”

10. **Resolve the Katyn hole by subtraction, not expansion.** Delete “One count belongs inside this chapter…” and the `KATYN EXHIBIT MERGES HERE` marker unless a finished exhibit already exists. Do not add another full ladder here: the chapter is roughly 6,500 words without it, and the next chapter moves directly into mass murder under occupation. If Katyn must appear, give it one tightly sourced consequences paragraph after 17 September and let a later chapter or note carry the comparison. As drafted, the orphan promise looks like a missing page and breaks the section numbering between 5 and 6.

11. **Make “Munich, still in service” do new work instead of replaying Acts I and II.** Keep the OpenStax blockquote whole, Truman, Korea, Suez and Vietnam; cut the paragraph beginning “Set the current editions side by side,” which repeats the US/RU/BY readings almost point for point. Then rewrite “a war happening this year,” “all of them in print now,” and “in the same school year” into edition-dated language that will survive publication. A 2022 analogy can be historically situated without pretending that editions from 2004, 2012, 2021, 2022 and 2023 occupy one synchronized present.

12. **Remove the deterministic claim about what classrooms do to adults.** Rewrite “Which way it is loaded … was settled years earlier, in whichever classroom the speaker sat in” and “neither speaker is quoting anything they were not taught.” The evidence shows that textbooks preserve rival uses of Munich; it cannot show that a reader’s later politics was settled by one textbook. Make the conclusion human-sized: **the two uses are available cultural scripts, and textbooks reveal how early each can acquire the authority of a lesson.**

13. **Compress “Credit where due” and “Where this stops being true” before Chapter 12 makes the same ceremonial turns again.** Give Lowe, bpb, Koshelev and Chubaryan one sentence each, focused only on contributions not already praised in the acts or tell. In limits, retain the syllabus boundary, the India exception and the three verbatim discrepancies; move the machine-translation workflow, extraction layout warning and newspaper provenance to receipts. Aim to remove 700–1,000 words overall. The chapter’s repeated praise and audit trail currently dissipate the momentum of the Munich analogy just before the Holocaust chapter opens with another facts–ladder–tell sequence.

14. **Add a precise handoff to the Holocaust before the required closing line.** After the compressed limits/receipts—or, better, after the thread if it becomes the final narrative section—add a sentence in substance: **“The arrows end at a divided Poland; the next chapter begins inside the occupations that followed, where borders, lists and administrative orders acquired human objects.”** Do not claim that Munich or the Pact caused the Holocaust. Do make the book’s movement from diplomatic language to enacted occupation legible, then retain “Now look up what your textbook calls it.” exactly.

15. **Repair the opening image’s visible metadata.** The caption/alt text says “Neville Chamberlaine,” attributes no actual photographer, and links to a visibly truncated Commons URL. Supply a clean human caption identifying Chamberlain and the paper, with the verified credit and working source link. This is the chapter’s first documentary object; it should not look like an unedited asset-database record.

**Verdict:** This chapter is an essay with a late exhibit inside it; promoting the six wiring diagrams into the main comparison would help it most.
